## Patterns

**Sector clusters among the winners.** The Buy band is dominated by three groups. First, **consumer
staples and grocery** — CCOLA (1), BIMAS (3), MGROS (4) — businesses that price with inflation,
carry net cash or close to it, and show real rather than merely nominal growth. Second, **regulated
or concession-like infrastructure and transport** — GRSEL (7), TAVHL (8), ENJSA (9) — where cash
flows are contracted and the discount is measured against a knowable asset base. Third,
**insurance** — ANSGR (5) at the top with TURSG (17) just behind — direct beneficiaries of a 37%
policy rate on the float. MPARK (6, healthcare), TUPRS (10, refining) and AKSA (2, chemicals) round
it out. Notably, **zero banks reached the Buy band** despite large discounts to book: GARAN, AKBNK,
ISCTR, VAKBN and YKBNK all cluster at 50-55 with strong Q and weak P — cheap franchises with broken
charts and sub-inflation real ROE.

**Sector clusters among the losers.** The bottom third is remarkably concentrated. **Small-cap
power / IPPs** recur throughout — ESEN (92), CANTE (96), ZOREN (87), IZENR (84), MAGEN (82), ODAS
(64) — nearly all sharing one shape: revalued assets, heavy debt or collapsed spreads, and a book
value that flatters. **Cement** is the second cluster: BSOKE (72), BTCIM (74), OYAKC (53), with only
CIMSA (27) still profitable enough to hold the Hold band. **Leveraged consumer durables and
chemicals** are the third — VESTL (95), ARCLK (71), SASA (73), PETKM (78) — all with net debt that
dwarfs the equity story. The **Pasifik-group complex** (PATEK 100, PAHOL 79, PASEU 63, PSGYO 54)
appears as a single correlated block, all low-confidence. And the **football clubs** (FENER 91,
GSRAY 89) fail on identical arithmetic: revalued-asset book values that no operating profit
supports.

**Speculative ceiling.** Thirty-six names scored **Q < 45** and were therefore subject to the
55-point cap, but the cap was **actually binding on only three**: **TKFEN** (37.7), **EFOR** (33.2)
and **MIATK** (24.9). The other 33 scored below 55 on their own merits — which is the design working
as intended: the ceiling is a backstop against price-driven redemption of weak businesses, not a
routine haircut. TKFEN is the textbook case — a +119% YTD takeover-driven chart attached to
loss-making operations at 1.9x fair value.

**Average Q vs P.** Mean **Q = 51.8**, mean **P = 40.6** — an 11.2-point gap, and the single most
important number in this scan. The BIST-100 as scored here is a market of *decent-to-mediocre
businesses at unattractive prices and bad moments*, not the reverse. Mean R was **0.907** (only 21
names carried a clean R = 1.00) and mean FINAL **40.2**, which sits inside the Speculative band.
Fully **56 of 100 names trade above their base intrinsic fair value**, with a median discount of
**−9.5%** — i.e. the median BIST-100 name is about 10% expensive. **No name scored Strong Buy.**

**The tape was overwhelmingly negative.** Only **4 names** earned the +7 confirmed-constructive tape
(CCOLA, AKSA, ENJSA, AEFES) against **21 names** carrying the −7 confirmed-freefall term — better
than 5:1. That is a market-wide risk signal in itself, and a large part of why so many
fundamentally sound businesses landed in the Speculative band.

**Divergence 1 — high Q, low P (the quality watchlist).** Good businesses at bad moments; per the
ruleset these are *waiting*, not failing. The clearest cases: **ASELS** (Q79 / P46 — the highest
Quality score in the entire scan, record $20.7bn backlog, but 44x trailing and ~12% above FV),
**ASTOR** (Q76 / P46 — 38% EBITDA margin and net cash, priced ~28% above a ₺230 FV), **GARAN**
(Q70 / P44.9 — best-in-class bank, ROE 28.8%, below all three MAs), **KTLEV** (Q68.9 / P36 — ROE
116% at PD/DD 21.8), **TSKB** (Q62 / P43 — cheapest bank with the highest ROE but in confirmed
freefall), **EUPWR** (Q62.7 / P40.8) and **GLRMK** (Q58.3 / P23.2 — the widest Q−P gap in the book
at 35 points), plus GENIL, ENERY, MAVI, ALTNY, ISMEN and GUBRF. Every one is a "trigger and wait"
name: the business is not the problem, the entry is.

**Divergence 2 — high P, low Q (the speculative side).** Far rarer this scan, which is itself
informative: **KRDMD** (Q47 / P57 — 0.34-0.50x book and a clean balance sheet against ~0% ROE),
**DOAS** (Q44.8 / P56.6), **SISE** (Q45.9 / P48.6 — a 0.50x book that reflects leverage, not value),
**DOHOL** (Q46.9 / P48.7 — effectively a macro option on TCMB easing) and **PSGYO** (Q41 / P48).
Here price and discount are doing the work while the business has not yet cooperated — tradeable in
small size, and never rateable as Buy while Q stays under 45. That only five names fit this profile,
against thirteen on the quality-watchlist side, says the current BIST is short of cheap-and-moving
setups and long on good-but-badly-timed ones.

## Caveats

1. **Single-date snapshot.** Every score is a point-in-time reading for **2026-08-02**. The v3.1
   backtest measured a ~4-point excess-return spread between the ≥65 and <35 cohorts over 63
   trading days, with mean cross-sectional IC ≈ 0.06 — modest but real. That is a *cohort* edge,
   not a per-name forecast; a single scan is not a prediction about any one stock.
2. **Judgment sub-scores are analyst opinion, not data.** Quality, Sector, Connections, News &
   Governance, Catalysts and every fair-value estimate are reasoned judgments. Only the technical
   sub-score is backtest-validated. Facts (tool outputs, dated figures) live in the per-symbol
   scorecards; everything downstream of them is opinion and can be argued with. Reasonable analysts
   will disagree by 10+ points on many Q scores.
3. **Technicals are from Friday's close (2026-07-31).** The market was closed for the entire scan.
   Any gap at Monday's open invalidates the entry, stop and target levels — and can flip a tape
   term in either direction, which is worth up to 14 points of FINAL on its own.
4. **Macro was held fixed at 58** for all 100 names so scores are comparable across the book. This
   is a deliberate simplification: no stock is rewarded or punished for a differing view of TCMB,
   TÜFE or the TRY path. It also means the whole table moves together if the macro read changes —
   treat cross-sectional *rank* as more reliable than absolute level.
5. **67 of 100 names carry a low-confidence flag**, overwhelmingly small and mid-caps where
   fundamentals were incomplete, thinly reported, or distorted by TMS-29 inflation accounting,
   revaluation-driven book values and thin liquidity. For those names, borderline stances should be
   read as the more cautious neighbouring band, per the ruleset.
6. **Inflation accounting distorts everything.** In a ~29-37% inflation regime nominal growth
   flatters, monetary gains can push net profit above EBITDA (TOASO is an explicit example), and
   revalued PPE inflates book value so PD/DD looks cheap when it is not (TUKAS, GSRAY, PSGYO).
   Where the scorecards adjust for this they say so; where data was insufficient the item was
   marked N/A and scored at its neutral midpoint.
7. **Extreme Disc% values are ratios, not percentages to be read literally.** Where a stock trades
   at several times intrinsic value (ODINE −3233%, IEYHO −3936%, KUYAS −2116%, PASEU −1450%,
   DSTKF −1161%), the number simply says "price is many multiples of the fair-value estimate". Those
   fair-value anchors are the least reliable in the book and belong to the lowest-confidence names.
8. **No trades. None were placed and none are recommended.** Ratings are information. Levels in the
   per-symbol files are conditional entries, stops and trim zones — not instructions — and any order
   would require a separate, explicit instruction naming symbol, side and quantity.
