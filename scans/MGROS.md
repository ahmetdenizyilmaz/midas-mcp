## Bull case
- **An effectively unlevered balance sheet in a 37% rate regime.** Net financial debt ₺7.6bn against FY2025 EBITDA of ₺35.1bn is ~0.22x. While SOKM, CRFSA and BIZIM bleed, Migros' negative-working-capital float earns at the policy rate. When cuts resume, it is the operator best positioned to buy share from distressed competitors rather than defend against them.
- **The peer discount is wider than the quality gap.** FD/FAVÖK 4.0x vs BİM's 9.7x and PD/DD 1.3x vs 2.5x, for the only other consistently profitable national grocer. Convergence toward even 5.5x EV/EBITDA implies ~₺876/share; three independent intrinsic anchors cluster at ₺780 base, ~20% above spot, with sell-side consensus (₺913.94, 14 analysts) sitting higher still.
- **Real, not nominal, growth.** FY2025 revenue of ₺454.2bn was ~+26% in real terms after deflating ~74% nominal growth by ~35-38% CPI — genuine share capture from 3,784 stores plus a compounding online channel, in the most defensive end-market on the exchange.

## Bear case
- **The margin has no cushion, and 2026 costs are already locked above CPI.** Net margin 1.62% and operating margin 2.53% meet a **36% collective wage increase against a 29.21% expected CPI**. Labour is the largest opex line; a modest miss on pass-through takes a disproportionate bite out of earnings, and the Q2 print in August is the first read.
- **Traffic is falling.** Management's own Q1 2026 framing was that digital growth *offset physical store traffic declines*, and the Q1 run-rate annualizes below the FY2025 pace. With a consumer squeezed by 37% rates, down-trading to hard discounters is the structural threat, and it shows up as volume before it shows up as revenue.
- **The sector destroys capital and price is politically capped.** Three of four listed comps are loss-making, CarrefourSA has negative equity, industry net margin is −0.60% — and "fahiş fiyat" scrutiny limits full cost pass-through. Add a ~3,784-store leased estate (LT debt/assets 0.80) and current ratio 0.34, and the operating leverage cuts both ways. Technically, price remains below both SMA20 and SMA50 with a still-declining SMA50 — the pullback is not yet resolved.

## Verdict
MGROS sits in the upper-right quadrant of the Q/P map — respectable quality (Q 62.9, anchored on a near-zero-leverage balance sheet and consistent profitability in a sector where most peers lose money) meeting a genuinely attractive price (P 64.6, ~20% below a ₺780 intrinsic base and at a 24-59% multiple discount to its only healthy comp). With a clean risk overlay (R = 1.00) and no tape adjustment either way, the blend lands at **63.8 — Buy / Accumulate**, not Strong Buy: the technical structure is a constructive base attempt, not a confirmed uptrend, and the 36% wage settlement is a real, quantified FY2026 margin headwind that the market has not yet seen tested.

The horizon this suits is **6-18 months** — long enough for the disinflation/rate-cut path to re-rate a low-leverage domestic-demand compounder, and for the peer discount to narrow. It is not a momentum trade: at ₺625.50 the reward-to-risk to the first resistance is only 0.79:1, so the discipline is to accumulate into the ₺610 support shelf where the geometry improves to ~2.2:1.

**What would change the stance.** *Upgrade toward Strong Buy:* a Q2 2026 print (due ~August 2026) showing the EBITDA margin held at or above ~7.7% despite the wage deal, plus a reclaim of SMA50 (₺657.76) on above-average volume — that combination would lift TechnicalTiming through 70, trigger the +7 tape term, and push FINAL into the low-70s. *Downgrade toward Hold:* an EBITDA margin miss below ~7% on continued traffic decline, or a break of the ₺610 three-touch support that carries price under SMA200 (₺597.93) and activates the freefall pattern — that would cost the technical sub-score 10-15 points and apply −7 tape, taking FINAL toward the mid-50s.
